Behind the music.

Another Can-Can is a set of eighteen newly rendered performances. Its eight composer-inspired variations are original arrangements, not compositions by the named composers. No live musician or historical recording is represented by the playback.

The historical theme

Jacques Offenbach (1819–1880), Galop infernal from Orphée aux enfers (1858). The historical piano reduction is Heugel et Cie., Paris, c. 1862, plate H. 3781, printed pp. 80–83. The composition and historical edition are public domain.

The machine-readable source is Noctave’s MusicXML edition, dedicated to the public domain under CC0 1.0. Its file and SHA-256 are retained in the repository. The canonical piano performance is an abridged historical excerpt; the orchestra preserves its outline and melody with new orchestration. Neither is the complete opera or complete original orchestral score.

BnF Gallica historical print · IMSLP catalogue

The new variations

Eight transformations use the Offenbach refrain as their source material. Their plans of meter, key, harmony, instrumental distribution and form were authored separately for this project. No musical themes by the eight namesakes were quoted. In particular, the Poulenc-inspired arrangement uses broad characteristics such as dry articulation, added-note harmony and lyrical contrast, not his protected melodies.

Written tempo curves, articulation, dynamics, melodic delays and pedal changes are part of the editable score. “Phrase-linked” switching preserves a cue and relative position between piano and orchestra. Between different composers it chooses a corresponding formal section: these are new compositions, not bar-for-bar replacements.

The piano

Salamander Grand Piano v3, sampled by Alexander Holm from a Yamaha C5, with FLAC remapping by kinwie. Licensed under Creative Commons Attribution 3.0.

The custom renderer selects twelve of the library’s sixteen velocity layers as required by these scores. Transformations include adjacent-pitch resampling, note and pedal releases, stereo blending and a custom room response. Attribution applies to the sampled piano audio, not merely the code.

The orchestra

GeneralUser GS 2.0.3 by S. Christian Collins, rendered with FluidSynth. Its license permits musical recordings, including commercial release. The author notes incomplete individual provenance for some raw samples. This project distributes rendered music, not the raw SoundFont.

The exact SoundFont, pinned upstream revision, download digests, and full license are documented in the repository. No sample library downloads happen in your browser.

Recording & mastering

All eighteen takes are 48 kHz stereo MP3s generated from the included MIDI/JSON scores. A single linked gain adjustment per take keeps comparisons reasonably consistent, without compression or limiting. The recordings measure approximately −19 to −17 LUFS, with decoded true peaks below −1.7 dBTP. Natural changes in phrase dynamics are retained.

Tests check source/recording hashes, durations, stereo format, clipping and silence. Technical tests do not establish human-performance realism or replace musical listening. These are expressive digital performances.

The website

Designed and built for Rocky as a self-contained, editable listening experience. Libre Bodoni by the Libre Bodoni Project Authors and Instrument Sans by the Instrument Sans Project Authors are self-hosted under the SIL Open Font License. The score visualizations are generated from the exact authored note events, rather than an unrelated decorative waveform.

No tracking, analytics, third-party runtime requests, account system or autoplay. Respect for reduced-motion preferences. Keyboard-accessible playback, cue jumps and native range controls.
