The experiment

Same spark.
Different instincts.

What makes a musical voice recognizable: the notes, or the choices between them?

Start with the exuberant dance from Orphée aux enfers. Keep its recognizable DNA, then change the musical thinking around it. Counterpoint instead of punchlines. A whispered answer instead of a shout. A rising orchestral surge instead of a single grand gesture.

Composed, not filtered

These are separate arrangements, not one recording run through nine effects. Harmony, register, texture, rhythm and instrumentation are authored for each interpretation.

Shaped, not randomized

Phrase-level tempo curves, purposeful accents, articulation and balance shape the performances. Licensed sampled instruments bring the written parts to life.

The eight composer-inspired variations are newly authored arrangements, not works by those composers. These are expressive computer-rendered performances, not recordings of live musicians. The Poulenc-inspired variation uses broad stylistic ideas, not quotations from his music.

The melody

Jacques Offenbach, Orphée aux enfers, “Galop infernal.” Abridged from the historical Heugel piano reduction, plate H. 3781 (Paris, c. 1862), pp. 80–83, using Noctave’s CC0 MusicXML edition. The underlying composition and historical edition are public domain.
